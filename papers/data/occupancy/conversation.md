# From embedding geometry to conserved class amounts

## Research question

Can a powered chart make distinctions in a language model's embeddings both resolvable and useful for recognizing an independently labelled mechanism? Paper 1 gives curves, frames and atoms. Paper 2 supplies matched nulls, Boltzmann shells and falsifiable verdicts. Paper 3 adds spherical harmonics, chart power and heat flow. Paper 4 distinguishes item count, distinguishable codes, certified resolution and packing. The papers README connects these instruments. Paper 5 asks how much corpus amount a frozen classifier assigns to each meaning.

## Embeddings and recognition

Cloudflare Workers AI embedded 108 newly authored field notes with BGE-M3. Each of 18 instruments supplied one note for each of six mechanisms. Six instruments furnished 36 references. Two disjoint sets A and B each supplied six unseen instruments. A second inference requesting mean pooling also reported CLS. Across passes 96 vectors matched exactly, minimum same-text cosine was 0.99999867 and all recorded top choices agreed. Labels and identifiers were excluded from input text.

The reference-fitted dilation on the unit sphere in 1,024 coordinates used power 2.6854. At the native reference median chord 0.9793, fixed-order greedy packing increased from 3 to 7 on A and 6 to 11 on B. At chord 0.095 all 36 ambient directions were already resolved. After rescaling temperature by the reference median-energy ratio 2.3422, pooled Gibbs recognition went from 21 to 22 and 23 to 26. The two-sided paired station-swap p-values were 1.0 and 0.5. Native centroids scored 24 and 25. Station removal plus Gibbs scored 23 on A against 16 native nearest neighbours, with paired p=0.1875. A separate six-bit semantic-frame experiment reached 7/36 on B versus 9/36 for matched PCA bits. These exploratory outcomes do not establish a general recognition advantage.

## Curvature, multiplicity and Fourier analysis

The spherical Pythagorean law gives cos(c)=cos(a)cos(b) for right geodesic legs on the unit sphere. In cosine-energy units it yields Ec=Ea+Eb−EaEb. Sarah Greenwald's string experiment illustrates the curved inequality. A control using two observed angular distances and an orthogonalized tangent gave a²+b²−c²=0.1677. Orthogonality of Fourier functions instead gives additive squared coefficients. Learned sampled frequencies can have a nonidentity Gram matrix. The proposed dyadic sum over 2^n states of m0/[2^n(n+1)] diverges for positive m0, whereas interpreting those numbers as amplitudes and squaring them yields convergent power. A note occupies one prefix cell at each depth; distinct levels re-represent the same observations.

The four-bit reference-frozen frame selected by the half-bit rule produced five test positions on A. Angular degree-1 power share changed from 0.285 to 0.202 and degree-3 from 0.326 to 0.376 under RMS power. Spherical Gibbs remained at 6/36. The Walsh transform on the binary cube reproduces Hamming Gibbs allocation. At degree j the multiplicity is choose(d,j); for r=exp(−1/T) the relative kernel multiplier is ((1−r)/(1+r))^j. Squared coefficients describe pair separations. Signed class-conditioned coefficients support recognition.

## The mole analogy and the measured conversion

A mole counts specified entities. Molar mass converts measured mass to amount. Multiplying (m/M)N_A by a normalized canonical level weight gives the expected molecular count at that level; R=N_A k_B equates per-molecule and per-mole Boltzmann exponents. In yubiOS each note apportions one unit across classes. Within-class normalization kept predictions fixed when the entire offset bank was replicated eightfold with frozen geometry; raw sums changed 16/36 decisions. Set B apportioned 36 note-equivalents and 4,856 UTF-8 text bytes. Drift received 6.817 note-equivalents and 928.88 bytes, or 136.25 bytes per assigned note-equivalent. That conversion is corpus-dependent. There is no universal physical molar mass for meaning.

## Evidence boundary

Packing, null spread, numerical tolerances, recognition and assigned byte totals are measurements on frozen observations. Algebraic identities, operator identities and unit conversions can be checked separately from those measurements. References to physical temperature, thermal equilibrium and statistical ensembles specify the analogy's assumptions. A useful external validation would freeze the instrument and evaluate independently collected fault records.
