## 1. Choosing the number of PCA/factor dimensions

There are three families of rules. Fixed thresholds include Kaiser's eigenvalue > 1 and the broken stick. Visual or heuristic rules include the scree test. Null-standardized rules compare observed eigenvalues with those of synthetic or permuted data: Horn's parallel analysis and its permutation version by Buja & Eyuboglu, now with theory from Dobriban. Likelihood and random-matrix rules (Minka; Gavish–Donoho) close the list. Paper 4's n* is closest to the null-standardized family, but its statistic is a count of binary classes, not an eigenvalue.

- **Horn (1965).** A Rationale and Test for the Number of Factors in Factor Analysis. *Psychometrika*. doi:10.1007/BF02289447. — (abstract) Estimates rank by subtracting the part of the latent roots attributable to sampling error, using randomly generated data. → The founding null-standardized dimension rule. Paper 4's z-scored axis gain applies the same idea to class counts instead of eigenvalues.  `[Horn1965]`
- **Kaiser (1960).** The Application of Electronic Computers to Factor Analysis. *Educational and Psychological Measurement*. doi:10.1177/001316446002000116. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) The source of the eigenvalue > 1 criterion (named 'Kaiser–Guttman' in Jackson 1993's abstract). → The fixed-threshold baseline. The ½-bit threshold in n* is likewise a fixed cut and inherits the same criticism unless calibrated against a null.  `[Kaiser1960]`
- **Cattell (1966).** The Scree Test For The Number Of Factors. *Multivariate Behavioral Research*. doi:10.1207/s15327906mbr0102_10. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) (The CrossRef abstract field holds only the citation.) The scree (elbow) test. → The ladder log2 N_d against d is a discrete analogue of a scree curve, and n* is an automated elbow rule.  `[Cattell1966]`
- **Buja & Eyuboglu (1992).** Remarks on Parallel Analysis. *Multivariate Behavioral Research*. doi:10.1207/s15327906mbr2704_2. — (abstract) Recasts parallel analysis as a permutation assessment that accounts for both finite-sample bias and sampling variability. → Closest classical analogue. Paper 4's column-permutation null permutes each column independently, which is Buja–Eyuboglu PA applied to the binarised matrix.  `[Buja1992]`
- **Velicer (1976).** Determining the Number of Components from the Matrix of Partial Correlations. *Psychometrika*. doi:10.1007/BF02293557. — (abstract) MAP: stop extracting components when the average squared partial correlation reaches its minimum, which gives an exact stopping point. → An alternative residual-dependence stopping rule worth reporting beside n* on the same map.  `[Velicer1976]`
- **Jackson (1993).** Stopping Rules in Principal Components Analysis: A Comparison of Heuristical and Statistical Approaches. *Ecology*. doi:10.2307/1939574. — (abstract) Compares heuristic rules (Kaiser–Guttman, bootstrapped variants, scree, broken stick, variance fraction) with statistical tests for the number of components. → A benchmark template, including the broken-stick rule, for comparing n* with classical rules.  `[Jackson1993]`
- **Minka (2000).** Automatic Choice of Dimensionality for PCA. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems*. OpenAlex W2133028937. — (abstract) Treats PCA as density estimation and uses Bayesian model selection to choose dimensionality. Reported as consistent with enough data, and more accurate and faster than cross-validation in simulations. → A likelihood-based comparator; it can be computed on the same corpus map.  `[Minka2000]`
- **Gavish & Donoho (2014).** The Optimal Hard Threshold for Singular Values is $4/\sqrt3$. *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*. doi:10.1109/TIT.2014.2323359. — (abstract) The AMSE-optimal hard threshold for singular values is (4/√3)√n σ when σ is known, or about 2.858 · median singular value when σ is unknown. → A modern random-matrix rule. Its unknown-noise form is median-referenced, a loose parallel to paper 4's median splits.  `[Gavish2014]`
- **Dobriban (2020).** Permutation methods for factor analysis and PCA. *The Annals of Statistics*. doi:10.1214/19-AOS1907. — (abstract) Studies parallel analysis, which scrambles each feature and keeps components whose singular values exceed those of the permuted data, and gives it a theoretical treatment (abstract truncated in the retrieved record). → Theoretical backing for the column-permutation null as a dimension-selection device.  `[Dobriban2020]`

## 2. Intrinsic-dimension estimation in ML

Beyond the estimators the paper already cites (Kégl, Levina–Bickel, Facco TwoNN, Camastra–Staiano), the main recent lines are angle/norm concentration (DANCo), multiscale local SVD, and layerwise ID profiles of deep and transformer representations. All of these output a real-valued ID. None outputs an integer dimension tied to a binary encoding.

- **Ceruti et al. (2014).** DANCo: An intrinsic dimensionality estimator exploiting angle and norm concentration. *Pattern Recognition*. doi:10.1016/j.patcog.2014.02.013. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) DANCo: an ID estimator based on concentration of neighbour angles and norms. A preprint exists as arXiv:1206.3881 under a slightly different title. → A real-valued ID baseline for the corpus map.  `[Ceruti2014]`
- **Little et al. (2017).** Multiscale geometric methods for data sets I: Multiscale SVD, noise and curvature. *Applied and Computational Harmonic Analysis*. doi:10.1016/j.acha.2015.09.009. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) Multiscale SVD: local PCA spectra across radii to separate intrinsic dimension from noise and curvature. → The closest analogue of paper 4's 'scale window', using singular values across scales where paper 4 uses class counts at ε.  `[Little2017]`
- **Ansuini et al. (2019).** Intrinsic dimension of data representations in deep neural networks. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2019)*. arXiv:1905.12784. — (abstract) The ID of trained-network representations is orders of magnitude below layer width. It first rises and then falls across layers, the last hidden layer's ID predicts test accuracy, and linear estimates miss these effects. → Empirical evidence for how ID evolves under successive re-encodings, relevant to the recursion question.  `[Ansuini2019]`
- **Pope et al. (2021).** The Intrinsic Dimension of Images and Its Impact on Learning. *International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2021)*. arXiv:2104.08894. — (abstract) Natural-image datasets have very low ID relative to pixel count, and lower-ID datasets are easier to learn and generalise better. → Precedent for reporting ID as a dataset-level property.  `[Pope2021]`
- **Valeriani et al. (2023).** The geometry of hidden representations of large transformer models. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2023)*. arXiv:2302.00294. — (abstract) In large transformers trained on protein-language and image-reconstruction tasks, representations evolve similarly across layers, and the data manifold expands to high dimension in the first layers. → A layerwise ID profile under iterated transformation, a second empirical anchor for recursion.  `[Valeriani2023]`
- **Tulchinskii et al. (2023).** Intrinsic Dimension Estimation for Robust Detection of AI-Generated Texts. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2023)*. arXiv:2306.04723. — (abstract) Proposes the ID of the manifold underlying a text sample's embeddings as a domain-robust invariant separating human from AI-generated text. → Direct precedent for ID of LLM text embeddings as a measurable corpus statistic.  `[Tulchinskii2023]`

## 3. Binary codes from (rotated) PCA projections

Learning-to-hash methods map data to Hamming-cube vertices by thresholding projections. The projections are random hyperplanes (LSH/SimHash), Laplacian eigenvectors (spectral hashing), PCA directions (the PCAH baseline), or PCA directions after an optimised rotation (ITQ). Median splits on PCA axes are a member of this family. It is the closest technical prior art for the map itself, although these works optimise retrieval quality, not a class count.

- **Weiss et al. (2008).** Spectral Hashing. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems*. OpenAlex W2293597654. — (abstract) Finding the best binary code is shown to be NP-hard. The spectral relaxation yields codes that are thresholded eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian, extended to new points via Laplace–Beltrami eigenfunctions. → Thresholding spectral coordinates into bits. Paper 4's median split of PCA axes is the linear-PCA member of this family.  `[Weiss2008]`
- **Gong et al. (2013).** Iterative Quantization: A Procrustean Approach to Learning Binary Codes for Large-Scale Image Retrieval. *IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence*. doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2012.193. — (abstract) ITQ finds a rotation of zero-centred (e.g. PCA-projected) data that minimises quantisation error to the vertices of a binary hypercube, by alternating minimisation linked to orthogonal Procrustes. The journal version of CVPR 2011 (10.1109/CVPR.2011.5995432; CrossRef-verified, reserve). → Closest prior art. ITQ treats the misalignment between PCA axes and the cube as something to fix by rotation. Paper 4 keeps the PCA axes, splits at medians and counts classes. Its alternation is also an alternating fixed-point scheme (cluster 7).  `[Gong2013]`
- **Wang et al. (2012).** Semi-Supervised Hashing for Large-Scale Search. *IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence*. doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2012.48. — (abstract) Notes that popular hashing methods (LSH, spectral hashing) build hash functions from random or principal projections, and proposes semi-supervised hashing. → Places principal-projection hashing as the standard baseline that paper 4's binary map coincides with, up to the threshold choice.  `[Wang2012]`
- **Charikar (2002).** Similarity estimation techniques from rounding algorithms. *Proceedings of the thiry-fourth annual ACM symposium on Theory of computing*. doi:10.1145/509907.509965. — (abstract) Defines LSH schemes with Pr[h(x)=h(y)] = sim(x,y), which yield compact sketches for similarity estimation and nearest-neighbour search. → The random-projection counterpart of paper 4's data-adapted bits, and a natural null for how much PCA axes add over random hyperplanes.  `[Charikar2002]`
- **Plan & Vershynin (2013).** Dimension Reduction by Random Hyperplane Tessellations. *Discrete & Computational Geometry*. doi:10.1007/s00454-013-9561-6. — (abstract) For K on the unit sphere, about O(w(K)^2) random hyperplanes (w = Gaussian mean width) give a uniform tessellation, and the sign-vector map embeds K into the Hamming cube with small distortion. → Theory for counting and controlling tessellation cells. It supplies a reference scale for N_d against which PCA-aligned splits can be compared.  `[Plan2013]`
- **Heo et al. (2012).** Spherical hashing. *2012 IEEE Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition*. doi:10.1109/CVPR.2012.6248024. — (abstract) Replaces hyperplane hash functions with hypersphere-based ones and a spherical Hamming distance. → A binary code built from spheres rather than half-spaces, in contrast with paper 4's half-space margins.  `[Heo2012]`

## 4. Fixed-margin null models for binary matrices

Ecology developed randomisations of presence–absence matrices that preserve row and/or column totals: checkerboard swaps, trial-swap, and Curveball. Gotelli's comparison shows that which margins are fixed controls Type I error. Paper 4's two nulls (curveball, fixed rows and columns; column permutation, fixed columns only) map directly onto this distinction.

- **Strona et al. (2014).** A fast and unbiased procedure to randomize ecological binary matrices with fixed row and column totals. *Nature Communications*. doi:10.1038/ncomms5114. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) The Curveball algorithm: fast, unbiased randomisation of binary matrices with fixed row and column totals. → The paper's primary null sampler.  `[Strona2014]`
- **Gotelli (2000).** Null Model Analysis of Species Co-Occurrence Patterns. *Ecology*. doi:10.1890/0012-9658(2000)081[2606:NMAOSC]2.0.CO;2. — (abstract) Compares nine null-model algorithms and four co-occurrence indices for Type I/II error. Algorithms that fix row sums are immune to Type I errors, and checkerboard counts are among the indices. → Justifies reporting both a fixed–fixed null (curveball) and a column-only null, and shows the choice of fixed margins matters.  `[Gotelli2000]`
- **Miklós & Podani (2004).** Randomization of Presence--Absence Matrices: Comments and New Algorithms. *Ecology*. doi:10.1890/03-0101. — (abstract) Shows that a trial-swap modification of the swap algorithm samples fixed-margin presence–absence matrices with equidistribution, and adds faster algorithms. → Uniformity guarantees for swap chains, relevant to the 20-draw curveball null.  `[Miklos2004]`
- **Verhelst (2008).** An Efficient MCMC Algorithm to Sample Binary Matrices with Fixed Marginals. *Psychometrika*. doi:10.1007/s11336-008-9062-3. — (abstract) A new MCMC sampler for binary matrices with fixed margins that converges much faster than earlier chains. → An alternative sampler and convergence reference for the fixed-margin null.  `[Verhelst2008]`
- **Carstens et al. (2018).** A unifying framework for fast randomization of ecological networks with fixed (node) degrees. *MethodsX*. doi:10.1016/j.mex.2018.06.018. — (abstract) Extends Curveball to unimode directed and undirected networks, with proofs that it is fast and unbiased (uniform), and gives Python/R code. → Formal unbiasedness of Curveball. It complements Carstens 2015, already cited.  `[Carstens2018]`

## 5. Geometry of language-model embeddings: anisotropy and isotropy

Contextual embeddings are strongly anisotropic. Post-processing that removes the mean and the top principal directions, or that whitens, improves similarity tasks. Isotropy has dedicated metrics (IsoScore). This bears directly on a method whose bits are the top PCA axes.

- **Ethayarajh (2019).** How Contextual are Contextualized Word Representations? Comparing the Geometry of BERT, ELMo, and GPT-2 Embeddings. *Proceedings of the 2019 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing and the 9th International Joint Conference on Natural Language Processing (EMNLP-IJCNLP)*. doi:10.18653/v1/D19-1006. — (abstract) Contextualised representations (ELMo, BERT) are not isotropic in any layer, and self-similarity of a word across contexts is low in upper layers. → Corpus embeddings are anisotropic, so the leading PCA axes may reflect common-mode structure rather than distinctions between documents.  `[Ethayarajh2019]`
- **Mu et al. (2017).** All-but-the-Top: Simple and Effective Postprocessing for Word Representations. *arXiv preprint*. arXiv:1702.01417. — (abstract) Removing the common mean vector and a few top dominating directions makes off-the-shelf word vectors perform better on lexical and sentence tasks. Published venue not confirmed in the sources checked; cited as arXiv. → Direct tension with paper 4, whose most informative bits are the top axes. Ablating axes 1–2 on the ladder would test this.  `[Mu2017]`
- **Su et al. (2021).** Whitening Sentence Representations for Better Semantics and Faster Retrieval. *arXiv preprint*. arXiv:2103.15316. — (abstract) The whitening operation improves the isotropy of BERT sentence representations, in the spirit of flow-based isotropy fixes. → Whitening rotates to the PCA axes and rescales each one. Median splits are invariant to per-axis monotone rescaling, so on the same axes whitening leaves paper 4's binary map unchanged (our inference).  `[Su2021]`
- **Rudman et al. (2022).** IsoScore: Measuring the Uniformity of Embedding Space Utilization. *Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2022*. doi:10.18653/v1/2022.findings-acl.262. — (abstract) Argues that existing isotropy measures (average random cosine, partition score) are inadequate, and proposes IsoScore for how uniformly a point cloud uses the ambient space. → A candidate isotropy statistic to report with N_d, before and after the lens.  `[Rudman2022]`

## 6. Spherical embeddings, stereographic projection and Möbius maps

Representation learning on S^{d-1} uses von Mises–Fisher models (clustering, hyperspherical VAEs) and the alignment/uniformity view of contrastive learning. Directional statistics provides Möbius-closed families with a stereographic link to Euclidean t-distributions. Learnable sphere diffeomorphisms (flows) and learnable curvature generalise a one-parameter 'lens power'.

- **Banerjee et al. (2005).** Clustering on the Unit Hypersphere using von Mises-Fisher Distributions. *Journal of Machine Learning Research*. OpenAlex W2145001205. — (abstract) Mixture-model clustering of L2-normalised (directional) data with von Mises–Fisher distributions, fitted by two EM variants. → The standard density model for corpus embeddings on S^{d-1}.  `[Banerjee2005]`
- **Wang & Isola (2020).** Understanding Contrastive Representation Learning through Alignment and Uniformity on the Hypersphere. *International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2020)*. arXiv:2005.10242. — (abstract) Identifies alignment of positive pairs and uniformity on the hypersphere as the two properties the contrastive loss optimises asymptotically, with an optimisable metric for each. → The uniformity metric is a natural objective with which to score lens powers s*.  `[Wang2020]`
- **Davidson et al. (2018).** Hyperspherical Variational Auto-Encoders. *Proceedings of the Thirty-Fourth Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence (UAI 2018)*. arXiv:1804.00891. — (abstract) Replaces the Gaussian VAE latent with a von Mises–Fisher distribution, giving a hyperspherical latent space that better captures hyperspherical structure. → Learned spherical latents as an alternative to a fixed stereographic lift.  `[Davidson2018]`
- **Kato & McCullagh (2020).** Some properties of a Cauchy family on the sphere derived from the Möbius transformations. *Bernoulli*. doi:10.3150/20-BEJ1222. — (abstract) A spherical Cauchy family that is closed under Möbius transformations of the sphere. Its parameter transforms by extended Möbius maps, and stereographic projection sends it to a multivariate t-family. The arXiv preprint has a different title. → The closest mathematical prior art to the 'lens power': a parametric family whose stereographic and Möbius behaviour is explicit.  `[Kato2020]`
- **Rezende et al. (2020).** Normalizing Flows on Tori and Spheres. *International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2020)*. arXiv:2002.02428. — (abstract) Expressive, numerically stable normalising flows on tori and spheres, built recursively on dimension from circle, interval or sphere flows. → Learnable sphere diffeomorphisms generalising a one-parameter lens.  `[Rezende2020]`
- **Skopek et al. (2020).** Mixed-curvature Variational Autoencoders. *International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2020)*. arXiv:1911.08411. — (abstract) A mixed-curvature VAE unifying Euclidean, elliptical (spherical) and hyperbolic latent spaces. → Learned curvature is a continuous analogue of choosing the lens power (our reading; the abstract excerpt does not mention stereographic models).  `[Skopek2020]`

## 7. Recursive / iterated embedding and fixed points

Treating structure as the fixed point of a remapping has firm precedents. Mean shift defines clusters as its fixed points. Lloyd's conditions characterise optimal quantisers as fixed points of a two-step alternation, and ITQ (cluster 3) is a rotation-constrained alternation of the same kind. Diffusion maps index a family of embeddings by the number of Markov steps. The data-processing inequality, and its strong form, bound what any re-encoding can retain about the original corpus.

- **Coifman & Lafon (2006).** Diffusion maps. *Applied and Computational Harmonic Analysis*. doi:10.1016/j.acha.2006.04.006. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) Diffusion maps: embeddings from eigenvectors of a Markov diffusion operator at a chosen time step. → The standard model in which repeated application of an operator defines a family of embeddings, and the formal template for 'remap the output'.  `[Coifman2006]`
- **Cheng (1995).** Mean shift, mode seeking, and clustering. *IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence*. doi:10.1109/34.400568. — (abstract) Generalises mean shift and studies its convergence. Cluster analysis becomes the deterministic problem of finding a fixed point of mean shift, and for Gaussian kernels mean shift is a gradient mapping. → Direct precedent for defining data structure as the fixed points of an iterated remap.  `[Cheng1995]`
- **Cover & Thomas (2005).** Elements of Information Theory. *Wiley*. doi:10.1002/047174882X. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) Standard reference for the data-processing inequality. → For a Markov chain corpus → Z_(k-1) → Z_k, I(corpus; Z_k) ≤ I(corpus; Z_(k-1)): no recursion step can add information about the corpus.  `[Cover2005]`
- **Raginsky (2016).** Strong Data Processing Inequalities and $\Phi$-Sobolev Inequalities for Discrete Channels. *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*. doi:10.1109/TIT.2016.2549542. — (abstract) The data-processing theorem bounds the output/input relative-entropy ratio by 1. For a fixed reference input the ratio can be strictly below 1 (strong DPI), and optimal SDPI constants for discrete channels are characterised. → If each re-freeze-and-re-power step has SDPI constant η < 1, retained information should decay like η^k (our inference), a testable prediction for the recursion runs.  `[Raginsky2016]`
- **Lloyd (1982).** Least squares quantization in PCM. *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*. doi:10.1109/TIT.1982.1056489. — (abstract) Derives necessary conditions for an optimal finite quantiser (Lloyd conditions). → The alternating fixed-point template (partition ↔ representatives) behind ITQ and k-means-style re-freezing.  `[Lloyd1982]`

## 8. Finite-scale dimension, Rényi information dimension and pattern counting (not already cited)

These items complement the paper's existing fractal bibliography. They cover practical estimation of dimension, finite-sample ceilings on resolvable dimension, the operational meaning of Rényi information dimension, and combinatorial bounds on the number of distinct binary patterns.

- **Theiler (1990).** Estimating fractal dimension. *Journal of the Optical Society of America A*. doi:10.1364/JOSAA.7.001055. — (abstract) Review of numerical methods for estimating fractal dimension of attractors and data. → A practical reference for finite-sample dimension estimation pitfalls.  `[Theiler1990]`
- **Eckmann & Ruelle (1992).** Fundamental limitations for estimating dimensions and Lyapunov exponents in dynamical systems. *Physica D: Nonlinear Phenomena*. doi:10.1016/0167-2789(92)90023-G. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) Fundamental limits on estimating dimension from finite data (the widely quoted ceiling is about 2 log10 N; not checked against the text here). → For N = 598 the ladder itself is capped at log2 598 = 9.22 bits. Both bound how many axes can be resolved.  `[Eckmann1992]`
- **Wu & Verdu (2010).** Rényi Information Dimension: Fundamental Limits of Almost Lossless Analog Compression. *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*. doi:10.1109/TIT.2010.2050803. — (abstract) The fundamental limit of almost lossless analog compression, under linear-compressor or Lipschitz-decompressor constraints, is Rényi's information dimension. → Gives the paper's D1 an operational reading as a compression rate.  `[Wu2010]`
- **Sauer (1972).** On the density of families of sets. *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A*. doi:10.1016/0097-3165(72)90019-2. — (metadata only: no abstract retrieved; the content note is from the title plus general knowledge and was not checked against the text) The Sauer–Shelah lemma: a family of VC dimension v realises at most Σ_(i≤v) C(d,i) patterns on d coordinates. → An upper bound on N_d: the distinguishability ladder is an empirical shatter function along nested, data-adapted half-spaces.  `[Sauer1972]`

## Closest prior art and what paper 4 adds

1. **Parallel analysis is a null-standardized dimension rule, and paper 4 shares its logic.** Horn [Horn1965], Buja & Eyuboglu [Buja1992] and Dobriban [Dobriban2020] already select dimensions by comparing observed spectra with permuted data. Paper 4's column-permutation null is, in mechanism, Buja–Eyuboglu permutation applied to the median-binarised matrix. What is new is the statistic (distinct bit-prefix classes N_d and their log-gain, rather than eigenvalues) and the addition of a null that fixes both rows and columns (curveball). The ½-bit threshold itself is a fixed cut in the Kaiser tradition [Kaiser1960]. The paper should present the null z-scores, not the threshold, as the inferential content.
2. **The binary map is a known hashing construction.** Thresholding PCA projections is the PCAH baseline of learning-to-hash [Wang2012], and spectral hashing [Weiss2008] thresholds spectral coordinates. ITQ [Gong2013] studies exactly the misalignment between PCA axes and binary-cube vertices and fixes it by a learned rotation. Paper 4 differs in three ways: it uses median (balanced) rather than zero thresholds, it does not rotate, and it measures class distinguishability rather than retrieval quality. Comparing N_d for PCA-median bits against ITQ-rotated and random-hyperplane bits [Charikar2002] would show whether these choices matter.
3. **Counting cells of a hyperplane tessellation has a theory.** Sauer–Shelah [Sauer1972] bounds the number of patterns, and Plan & Vershynin [Plan2013] give tessellation sizes for random hyperplanes in terms of Gaussian mean width. The distinguishability ladder is an empirical shatter function along nested, data-adapted half-spaces. Framing n* through these bounds is, to our knowledge, not done in the surveyed works (provisional: limited to this survey's sources).
4. **Intrinsic dimension: real-valued estimators versus an integer, encoding-tied n\*.** Packing, MLE, TwoNN, DANCo [Ceruti2014] and multiscale SVD [Little2017] output a real ID. Paper 4 outputs an integer prefix length defined by a code, and its Rényi D0/D1/D2 at finite scale have an operational compression reading via Wu & Verdú [Wu2010]. The overlap is that n* and D1 should be reported alongside a standard estimator (TwoNN, MLE) on the same map. The difference is that n* is defined on the code, not on neighbour distances.
5. **Finite-sample ceilings.** With 598 inputs the ladder is capped at log2 598 = 9.22 bits, and finite-data dimension limits [Eckmann1992; Theiler1990] apply to any scale-window estimate. This is already implicit in paper 4. Stating it explicitly would pre-empt reviewers.
6. **Fixed-margin nulls are transferred from ecology.** Curveball [Strona2014; Carstens2018] and swap chains [Miklos2004; Verhelst2008] are standard. Gotelli's [Gotelli2000] distinction between nulls that fix one margin and nulls that fix both maps onto the paper's two nulls. The close agreement between the paper's curveball and column-permutation results indicates that row margins carry little of the axis-gain signal. That is a substantive, reportable finding, not merely a robustness check.
7. **Anisotropy cuts against using the top axes as bits.** All-but-the-top [Mu2017] and the anisotropy findings [Ethayarajh2019] suggest that leading PCs of LM embeddings can be common-mode directions. Paper 4 relies on those axes as its first bits, so an ablation (dropping axes 1–2) and an isotropy score [Rudman2022] would address the obvious objection. Whitening [Su2021] does not change median bits on fixed axes (our inference).
8. **Sphere, stereographic lift and Möbius lens.** vMF modelling [Banerjee2005; Davidson2018] and hypersphere uniformity [Wang2020] are established. Kato & McCullagh [Kato2020] give a Möbius-closed spherical family with an explicit stereographic link to multivariate t. Choosing a single stereographic scale ('lens power') to maximise the certified class count at fixed chord, and comparing the deployed 0.9/max r rule with RMS rescale, has no direct precedent in the surveyed works (provisional). Uniformity [Wang2020] is the nearest objective to benchmark s* against.
9. **Not covered here.** Certification by half-space margins under ℓ∞ perturbation (the certified-robustness literature) and Lean formalisation of the counting identities were outside the requested clusters. No prior art was surveyed for them, and the 'what is new' claims there are unassessed.

## Gaps the recursion question touches

1. **Empirical layerwise analogues exist; a fixed-point theory for re-embedding does not (in this survey).** Layerwise ID profiles [Ansuini2019; Valeriani2023] show ID rising and then falling under repeated learned transformations. None of the surveyed works studies the specific map 'PCA frame → stereographic lift → re-freeze frame → re-power lens' or its fixed points.
2. **Fixed-point precedents are local and alternating.** Mean shift [Cheng1995] defines structure as fixed points. Lloyd [Lloyd1982] and ITQ [Gong2013] alternate between a partition and its representatives and converge to local optima. A recursive pipeline that re-freezes the frame is a similar alternation, so convergence to a fixed frame and lens should be expected only locally and should be checked numerically. The RMS rescale is idempotent by construction (rescaling to unit RMS twice equals once), so a fixed point is plausible whenever the re-frozen PCA frame of the lifted points reproduces itself (our inference).
3. **Information versus class count.** The data-processing inequality [Cover2005] guarantees that information about the original corpus cannot grow across recursion steps. The class count N at a fixed ε can still rise, because re-powering changes the effective resolution. The DPI bounds I(corpus; Z_k), not N_d. In addition, a deterministic map of a finite point set cannot increase the number of distinct points (≤ 598 here), so any growth in N at fixed ε reflects rescaling, not new information.
4. **Strong-DPI decay as a testable prediction.** If each step behaves as a channel with SDPI constant η < 1 [Raginsky2016], retained information should decay geometrically, roughly like η^k (our inference). A plot of log N_k or of mutual information with the step-0 classes against k should then be close to linear before saturation.
5. **Iterated diffusion as the closest formal model.** Diffusion maps [Coifman2006] index embeddings by the number of operator steps. Treating each recursion as one application of a (non-linear) operator would let the paper borrow the multiscale language ('t-step geometry') rather than invent it.
